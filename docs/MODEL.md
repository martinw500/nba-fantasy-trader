## 1. The matchup model and its calibration

- 9-cat advice (trade, ideas, waivers, standings) runs on the matchup model: `week` (per-game line × GAMES_WEEK × avail, missed games half-filled at waiver level) → `cat_probs` → `_score` → `outlook()`. `_score` is the share of categories won (`settings.scoring` "categories", the default and Martin's league) or `match_prob` ("one_win"). This week's chance to win the matchup (M9, Nov 5) has the categories move together: `match_prob(p, cats, WEEK_LOAD)` loads each category's margin 0.5 on one shared factor (counting stats +, TO −, FG% / FT% 0; probit, `phinv()`, 5 Gauss-Hermite nodes `GH`), so each category's own odds stay and a categories league's numbers don't move; only `this_week()`'s `win`, `add_drop()`'s week `win` and, in One Win, `_wscore()` (streams, the add plan, `_reserve()`, Start / sit, Try a move's week) use it. Season-long `_score` keeps the categories independent (on an average week the factor measured better in 1 of 4 seasons, bins worse in 3, and a One Win search would run ~9 → 35 s). `python backtest.py corr` fits it (real schedule, matchup log loss better every season, also with the load fitted on the other three); `report()` prints both. `season_sim()`'s `WEEK_SD` is a related shift (every category the same way, TO included, fitted on playoff odds), left separate. Deltas are in win-% points, `unit: "win%"`. Win-% cutoffs (`TIERS`, `VERDICT`, `MOVE`, `GUARD`, `accept()`) are in One Win terms, scaled by `League.s` (`CAT_SCALE` 0.4 in categories). z-scores (`value()`) are only for player tables and candidate selection.
- DISPERSION, SCHED_CV and PCT_NOISE are fitted by `python backtest.py` (2025-26 box scores, 20 simulated leagues, predictions from what was known each Monday, regulars who sat their team's last game out until a noisy return, `RETURN_NOISE`). Re-run it after changing projections (`baseline()`, `prior()`, `ros()`, availability) and keep the calibration tables near the diagonal. GAMES_WEEK is still a guess (STATUS_AVAIL's SUSP too). FILL (0.5) was one until Nov 16: `python backtest.py fill` counts every game a rostered player missed on a snapshot's day as his team stood that morning (parked in an IL slot with the active spots full, so his replacement plays; in an active slot on a night 10 active teammates played; or not covered; players who hadn't played yet this season left out, since `vec()` counts a parked player exactly): **0.47 of 245 missed games covered** (0.36 by an IL move, 0.11 lineup full anyway), 0.20 of first missed games and 0.76 from the 6th on, so 0.5 stays. It is a manager's habit more than a constant: 0.11 (Mathmagicians) to 0.93 (fatty zhou, 14 of 15; Martin: "if he gets injured i can js ir him and pick pj up after"). One number for every team is the known miss (ROADMAP M31, not built: at 0.9 for fatty zhou alone his season win % reads 59.1 → 64.9 on a 15-game sample, while Washington for Suggs only goes −0.21 → −0.36 and Suggs stays among his three cheapest). After any change, check standings stay realistic: about 36–59% on the real league as categories since A5 (Nov 7: Mathmagicians 35.8%, 35.3% before A6/A7; 37–59% after N9's availability, 41–57% after N5's IL slots; 37–63% before them; 19–80% as One Win back then). The snake-drafted demo is tighter (~47–52%).
- Category odds are calibrated, so cutoffs live in odds terms: `WEAK` (35%) = weak spot / punt hint, and This week's Ahead/Behind calls use the same cutoff.
- Punts concede the category (`_concede` sets your odds to 0) for your side only. Opponents always use real odds. This week doesn't concede (B10, Nov 6, Martin: Yahoo scores the matchup on every category): `_matchup()` and `_week_plan()` are real odds, so its win, score, streams, plan, Start / sit and Try a move's week; the punt still picks who's cut for a player back (`_week_roster()` → `_il_cut(punt)`) and every season number.
- `build()` = a team's weak spots (categories it wins <35% of weeks, `WEAK`). Points-mode trades value opponents on it.

## 2. Projections and player values

- Values are per game, rest of season: `ros()` = minutes (`GP/(GP+MIN_SHRINK)`, 3: fast, roles change) × production per minute (this season's minutes played vs `SHRINK` games of baseline minutes, slow; since A5, Nov 6, each stat at its own pace: `RATE_K` FGA 5, AST 15, STL 60, the rest `SHRINK`, all × k / `SHRINK` for rookies and `HYPE`; FG% / FT% at `PCT_K` 40 / 60 games of baseline attempts, makes = attempts × %, PTS = 2 FGM + 3PM + FTM; shooters 5+ points off their prior after 3–4 weeks keep 1–25% of the gap, the one k assumed 36–40%), on this season's line from `recent()`: box scores with each game weighted 0.5^(days ago / `RECENT` 45), GP the real count (fetch()'s line without box scores, or where their games don't equal fetch()'s GP; `gamelogs()` skips games still being played). Regulars and lifts read that same line. `had()` weighs the lifts players had the same way. `SHRINK` was picked replaying 2025-26 (Oct 27); `MIN_SHRINK` and `RECENT` on 2022-23 to 2025-26 (`python backtest.py recency`, Oct 30; rerun Nov 4 after M26 and A2: vs the old line weeks 1–6 +1.3 / −0.8 / +0.1 / −0.2%, season −0.4 / −1.4 / −0.3 / −1.3%; `MIN_SHRINK` 4 edged 3 on the season by 0.04–0.32% but the weekly matchup backtest, `python backtest.py [season]`, was worse at 4 and 6 in three of four seasons, so both stay). That is This week's line (`p_week`). An expert row with no numbers at all is no row, not a zero line (ESPN's 2023-24 file had 43, mostly that season's rookies). ## 4. Teammates' injuries

- Teammates' injuries (`lifts()`): regulars are players in `League.here` (a game this season or an ESPN listing; before any games, everyone): one who's neither has left the league, not sat out (`had()` took up to 9 min a game off his old teammates for Westbrook and co.), and the market ranks him last. While a regular (`REGULAR`+ min) with an ESPN return date sits, `SHARE` (0.5) of his minutes go to teammates in the rotation by room under `ROOM` (a cap: the rest goes to the bench) and role similarity, plus `USAGE` of the shots, points, assists and turnovers those minutes don't replace. Everyone out at once sits together (M4): a team's players out with a return date form stretches between their returns (`stretches()`), and in each one nobody out takes minutes and the sitters share the room, most minutes first. Each team's own split (M13, `onoff()`, `League.adj`): per receiver, a ridge of his minutes this season on which of its regulars sat (`sat()`, `recent()`'s weights), each sitter's effect pulled toward the rule's minutes as if they were `ONOFF_K` (4) more games, moves the rule's minutes (within `ROOM`, never below zero); minutes only, shots stay on the rule (Fears: +1.3 → +2.7 min while Murray sits). A sitter out since before the season (no games in it) was in the preseason projection, so `priced_in()` cuts his lift by the expert line's part of the receiver's minutes. `ros()` reads this season net of `had()` (the lifts players had in games regulars sat, those out together counted together), so a boost isn't counted twice: all of it This week, `NET_SEASON` (0.5) of it in the season line (A6: absences recur and availability already counts the starter's missed games, so netting all of it ran season lines low; next week, all of it is right). `_boost()` adds each stretch's lift for each player's games in it: rest of season in `p`, this week in `p_week` (`week_vec()` reads it). Start / sit (`_week_plan`) instead plays each game on its own day's stretch: the line less the averaged lift (`League.ahead`, or `_boost(week_end)` this week) plus that day's `self.lift` rows, so the games sum to the same week. `League.__init__`, `had()` and `backtest.py`'s `lifted()` / `season_parts()` must stay the same maths. Lifts only make sense with the injured player sitting; otherwise his minutes count twice. Score `backtest.py teammates` on full weeks: players who miss games that week run 3.6 min under their line, which availability covers.

## 5. Rosters: IL slots, open spots, who gets cut

- IL slots (N5): `League.il` = {team: players parked in IL / IL+ / NA slots}, `il_n` slots per team (`settings.il`). A parked player holds no active spot, so he counts through `_return()`, not `week`: no cover for his missed games, and from his return (`back`, share of games left) he takes the spot of his team's weakest active player on `effective()` (the best one back pushes out the weakest; nothing if he wouldn't beat him: he's the one cut). Nov 1: every real team's weakest was below the waiver level the model used before, standings moved −0.9 to +1.4 points. `vec()`, `_gains()` (Waivers, free moves) and `search()` (per deal: who leaves, gets cut, arrives or is picked up) all go through `_return()`; `_contrib()` alone stays additive (it only ranks drops). Injured free agents park too (B5, Nov 4: mchc allows adding straight to IL, Yahoo's default doesn't): `_stash()` is the one rule (OUT and a free IL slot), and with it his only move is `PARK` (no drop; `_drops(team=)` offers it, `_gains()` scores it through `_return()`, he can't take an active spot), the same in `free_moves()` / `after_free()` (the copy's `il` holds him), Waivers, Hot & cold and `add_drop()`, whose This week plays him from his return (`_week_roster()` on the roster after the move) as anyone parked. A free move's drop can be the `PARK` string: anything reading `(add, drop, gain)` tuples skips it like `None` (`_moves_out()`, Strategy's `_step()`: it raised KeyError, Nov 4). Trades keep each side's number of *active* players: injured players coming in park in free IL slots (longest out first), sending a parked player frees no active spot, and an injured player coming in is never the drop. `_after()` and `search()` do the same maths; keep them agreeing (test_model checks it). ## 6. Trades and the market

- `trade()` rejects players you don't own or that come from more than one team (KeyError). Keep that check, since AI tool input comes through it. `trade(drop=)` (Nov 13, Martin: "i wanna pick drops too"): your own cut to fit the deal, over `_after()`'s rule (they go first, the rule fills what's still needed, a spot they open beyond that gets the best free agent; never a player in the deal or off your roster, and your free moves keep them). The finder and the partner's side stay on the rule.
- Trade acceptance runs on the market (N0), never the model's own values: `League.market` = Yahoo Pre/Cur rank blended by `GP/(GP+CHASE)` (20, market only; Hot & cold alone uses `hot_market` at `HOT_CHASE`, don't let it leak into trades) as a geometric mean (so cold and hot starts move a player alike), re-ranked (model's perceived rank when Yahoo has none, `yahoo` False), valued `100 × MARKET^(rank−1)`. Players traded together count as a package (`package()`: the best in full, each next one at `PACKAGE` (0.7) times the one before), so consolidating (a 3-for-1 for a star) costs more than the market sum (Martin, Oct 30). `priced()` carries the plain sums as `sum` for display (Nov 1); acceptance, tiers and ranking read the package; `_priced()` and `search()`'s `stack()` both use it. A deal's market (`priced()`) nets out each side's drops (at their market value) and open spots (each `League.spot`, the waiver level's market value fixed at load, never the pickup's own rank: on a free-move copy the best "free agent" can be your own just-dropped Kessler), the same ones the matchup model makes, so `search()` and `trade()` agree. `TIERS` are (your win %, theirs, market gap range); fair is the market within 15% either way, and hard also passes a gap up to `FAIR_FIT` your way with both gaining (Oct 28: in fair, both ways, those read as unfair). `accept()` / `accept_level()` read the market gap; hard and finesse rank by it, then your gain. A deal never includes a player the side taking him would cut on arrival (`search()` skips it, `trade()` flags it), and a team below `roster_n` active players takes an extra one without a drop (`_room()`, in `_after()` and `search()` alike).
- Uneven trades always subtract `n * replacement()`. Replacement = top-5 FA average.
- 9-cat `trade()` and `search()` score on top of `after_free()`: your `free_moves()` (greedy add/drop or open-spot add while one clears `MOVE`; never dropping a trade chip, a market rank in the first `CHIP` rounds, `chips()`, M3: Waivers says "sell him instead"; a trade's forced drop is never one while anyone else can go either, M24: `_after()` and `search()`'s `low()` put chips last, `trade()` flags a deal that only fits by cutting one and `search()` skips it) are made first, so a deal gets no credit for a free pickup. Your pickups are never offered, and the players a deal sends are never dropped (`keep`). Each move is judged against the snapshot as the earlier moves left it: **shown** (every player is where the move put him) is dropped from the file by `load()`, which is how a new snapshot clears a move without a click; **stale** (the snapshot was pulled after the move's `at` and doesn't show it) is not applied, since the snapshot wins, and is kept for the banner's "isn't in your new snapshot"; otherwise **applied**: drops go to the free agents, activate / park change the slot, a trade swaps the players (in on the bench), adds come off the free agents onto the bench, park last (so an injured add or trade piece can go to an IL slot). A part that can't be made on this snapshot is skipped, never an error: the app has to load. `plan: true` is a move picked but not made (Who to drop's **Use this**): applied the same way, state `plan`, and it stays on a newer snapshot while every part can still be made; the app keeps one pick at a time. `League.moves` = moved()'s notes (`text` from `move_text()`, `state` made / plan / stale), in `state()["moves"]`, `/api/stamp` and the CLI's `roster_moves`. Like player what-ifs, only `app.py` and `main()` read them.
