---
name: gm
description: Fantasy basketball GM for the user's Yahoo league. Use when the user asks about their fantasy team, trades, who to drop or pick up, waivers, punting categories, sell-high/buy-low, or another team's roster. Pulls fresh league data and answers from model.py numbers.
argument-hint: "[question, e.g. who should I drop?]"
---

Answer the user's fantasy question with numbers from this repo's engine. Never invent values.

0. Run Python exactly as `.venv/bin/python` (no `source`, no bare `python`), as written below.
1. Data: if `data/league.json` is older than ~12 hours or the user says rosters changed, import the newest league snapshot in the repo folder (the `*snapshot*.txt` with the latest date in its name):
   `.venv/bin/python yahoo.py import <that file>`. If there's none, say so and use the existing `data/league.json`, or `.venv/bin/python yahoo.py demo` if that doesn't exist either.
2. Run what the question needs (all print JSON; add `--punt "FT%,TOV"` when the user punts):
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py team` — category ranks, H2H vs each team, punt suggestions, roster values
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py trade --give "A, B" --get "C"` — evaluate a specific trade. 9-cat also returns `cats_detail` (both teams' category totals and odds, before → after), `vs`, `standings` (`rival` = partner or a top-4 team gaining more than the user), `flags`, `build_after` (punt hint), `market`, `tiers` (finder difficulties it clears) and `accept`. It's scored on top of the waiver moves the user could make without a trade (`free`), and so is every finder result; the other team is judged on the trade alone. Say so, e.g. "compared with just adding Vučević for Achiuwa (+1.2)"
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py ideas --mode fair|finesse [--team "Name"]` — trade finder
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py search --get "Target" | --give "Mine" [--lock "A, B"] [--tier easy|fair|finesse|hard] [--goal cats --cats "STL,AST" | --goal team --vs "Name"] [--size 3] [--healthy] [--shape consolidate|depth] [--max-overpay 10]` — targeted search: offers for a player, buyers for yours, deals that fix categories or beat one team. Each result has `market` (how managers price it from Yahoo's ranks: give / get in market value, `diff_pct`, a verdict like "you overpay 35%") and `accept` (Likely yes / Maybe / Long shot, mostly on market). Tiers: easy = they win on market, fair = within 15% (30% when both gain), finesse = even or their way but the fit is yours, hard = you win on market
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py waivers` — best free agents + add/drop moves (`drop: null` = an open roster spot), and `free`: the moves trades are scored on top of
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py movers` — sell high / buy low: value over the last 14 days (`form`, `form_gp` games) vs rest of season (`gap`). Every player dict carries `form` and a `trend` (up / down) when the gap is 3+
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py week [--vs "Name"]` — this week's matchup: `score` (expected categories won–lost), `win` (chance to win more categories), per-category totals so far → projected, odds and `call` (safe / chase / lost / punt), `streams` (free agents scored on this week's win %, with `drop`, `day` = the best day to make the add, the drop playing until then, and `season` = season-long change), `adds` (`used` / `max` / `left` this matchup week: the league allows 4; null = no cap) and `plan` (the best way to spend the adds left: `moves` in order, each `day`, `add`, `drop` (a later one can drop an earlier streamer), `gain`; `keep` = the streamers still on the roster at the end, `season` = what that costs or gains over the season). No adds left = no streams. Quote the adds left with any stream advice, and with one left say which day to save it for. `opponent: null` means none is saved: ask who the user plays and pass `--vs`
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py strategy` — "should I punt?" (~15s; `--size 2` is faster): each build (no punt, every category, six pairs) with its plan in `steps`: waiver adds first (`free`), then up to three fair trades in a row, each searched with that punt on the rosters the trade before left. Every step has `win` (true win % after it, nothing conceded) and `add` / `drop` (its net waiver moves). A trade step also has `trade` (`team`, `give`, `get`, `their_net`, `market`, `accept`, and `later`: it needs the earlier trades first). A plan's first trade can lower true odds when later ones pay it back, which is how a punt pays off. `builds` is ranked by `win` (after the best stretch of the plan), `gain` is vs today's `win`, `pick` is the build to play (`[]` = stay balanced unless a punt beats it by more than a toss-up), and `edge` is the top build's lead over balanced. Balanced also tries every other build's trades and repeats each build's plan while every trade lifts true odds, so a punt only wins through moves it alone unlocks. Each build also has `stars` (your top five players' weak spots it concedes, z ≤ −1) `buy` (players on other teams the market prices furthest below their worth to the build, with `fit_rank` vs `mkt_rank`) and `sell`, its mirror on your team (5+ market points over his worth to the build), each with `why`: Yahoo projects him higher than we do, a hot start (Cur ahead of Pre), games missed, or his value sits in the punted categories. `team` also returns `cheap`: categories ranked by `per_z`, the odds gained from one more player-z
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py compare --players "A, B"` — 2 to 4 players side by side ("who do I keep / start / trade for"): each one's player card (value, `form`, per-game `lines`, `weeks` = NBA games this matchup week and the next three, `avail`, market and `fit_rank` on the build) plus `slot`: the user's season win % with him in one of their roster spots (`delta` vs today's `win`; the compared players the user owns all leave, `cut` = he wouldn't make the roster, `add` / `drop` = who fills or loses a spot). Other teams' rosters stay as they are, so it isn't a trade result. `best` names who's ahead on each number
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py move --add "A" [--drop "B"] [--vs "Team"] [--day YYYY-MM-DD]` — one add/drop the user names ("should I grab X for Y?"): `net` = change in season win % (`win` → `win_after`, `rank` → `rank_after`), `week` = this week vs the saved opponent (`net`, `win` and `score` before → after, `day` = when it's made: `--day`, else its best day, the drops playing until then; `games.add` = his starts left this week, from `claim` when he's on waivers; `games.drop` = the starts the drops lose; null without an opponent), `adds` = adds used and left this week, `capped` = none left (the move waits for next week, `week` is null), `cats` = each category's season and week odds before → after, `verdict`. Scored on today's roster like `waivers` (not on top of the other free moves). No `--drop` = an open spot, else whoever the user would miss least; `drop` entries with `auto` were dropped for room (dropping a player in an IL slot frees no active spot), `add.parks` = he goes into a free IL slot, `open` = active spots the move leaves empty. A move that helps this week but costs the season is a stream: say so
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py schedule` — schedule planner ("when do I stream", "who has a big schedule"): `weeks` = the rest of this matchup week and the next three, each with `days` (`players` = the user's starters that day, best `lineup` with a game; `sit` = players with a game who'd sit; `open` = empty starting slots while the NBA plays, room to stream; `nba` = NBA teams playing) and week totals (`games`, `starts`, `sit`, `open`). `roster` = each active player's `sched` (games and starts per week), `il` = players parked in IL slots (not counted), `adds` = adds used and left this week (every later week starts with `max`). `teams` = every NBA team's `weeks` (games), `open` (games on the user's open days, i.e. starts a streamer from there would get), `total`, `playoffs` (games in each fantasy playoff week, listed in `playoffs`), `mine` and `fa` (best healthy free agent there on the build). Counts are today's roster; positions are ignored
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py roster --team "Name"` — any team's roster
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py sources` — when each downloaded source was last fetched (`fetched`, `age` in hours, `hours` = its refresh window): `status` ok / stale / failed (the download failed, this is the old copy) / missing (none: the numbers leave out `lost`). Check it when numbers look off or the user asks how current the data is, and say so when injuries or box scores are an old copy
   - `.venv/bin/python model.py news` — ESPN's fantasy news on the user's players (`mine`) and their NBA teammates in the rotation since the snapshot (at least two days), newest first; `new` = after the snapshot. News never moves a number: use it to explain one (a return date, a minutes change) or to flag what the model can't see yet
3. Answer like a blunt GM: the move, the number, which categories change, the risk (injury, role, small sample early in the season). For trades always mention the market verdict (`market.verdict`) and what it does for the other manager (`their_net`); `their_view` is only how it reads on hot and cold starts.
4. If the user wants a message to send another manager, write it short and casual, then run it through the `humanizer` skill.

In 9-cat, `net` / `gain` / `fit` are changes in the user's win % vs the league (percentage points). The user's league is Head-to-Head Categories (`settings.scoring: "categories"`): win % is the share of categories won in a typical week, which is how the standings count, so +1% is about 0.1 categories a week. In a One Win league it's the chance to win the weekly matchup. They come from a matchup model with both rosters updated, so they already account for the user's strengths and weaknesses, every opponent's roster, roster-size drops and adds, and games-played and injury risk (`avail`). Lead with those numbers and the projected finish (`rank` → `rank_after`), and use player `value` (per-game z-scores) only to explain why. Player lines blend an expert projection (`proj`: Yahoo's from the snapshot, else ESPN's, 60%) with the last two seasons, then lean on this season as games pile up. While a regular teammate is out with a return date, a player's lines include his share of that teammate's minutes and shots for the games before the return (`lift`: `by`, `return`, `games`, per-game `MIN` and stats; all of This week's games, a share of the rest of season). Mention it when it explains a pickup or a stream ("LaMelo gets +0.7 min while Edwards is out, back Dec 21"). A category the user can't win or already dominates barely moves the odds, which is the reason to punt it.
